# Understanding Pitru Paksha

Pitru Paksha (the "fortnight of the ancestors") is a 16-lunar-day period in the Hindu calendar dedicated to honoring deceased ancestors (*Pitrus*). It occurs during the waning phase of the moon (*Krishna Paksha*) in the lunar month of Bhaadrapada.

## Core Significance
In Hindu tradition, three generations of ancestor souls reside in *Pitru Loka*, a realm between Heaven and Earth. During this period, the barrier between realms thins, allowing spirits to visit the earth. Honoring them during this time fulfills the *Pitru Rina* (debt to ancestors).

## Key Rituals
*   **Tarpan:** Offering water mixed with black sesame seeds, barley, and kusha grass to refresh and honor the ancestors.
*   **Pinda Daan:** Offering balls of cooked rice, milk, ghee, and sesame seeds representing physical sustenance.
*   **Annadaan (Food Donation):** Sharing prepared food with priests, the broader community, and animals (especially crows, cows, and dogs).
*   **Sattvic Living:** Maintaining a simple, vegetarian lifestyle and postponing major celebrations or luxury purchases to focus on remembrance.

## Important Days
*   **Maha Bharani:** Honors those who passed away in the preceding year.
*   **Avadhava Navami:** Dedicated to honoring mothers and female ancestors.
*   **Sarvapitri Amavaasya (Mahaalaya):** The final new moon day, serving as a universal remembrance for all ancestors, even those whose exact passing dates are unknown.
